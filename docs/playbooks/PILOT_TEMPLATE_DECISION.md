# Pilot Template Decision

## Purpose

This document captures the decision after two pilot rewrites:

- `intro-m1-l1-what-is-ai`
- `creator-m1-l1-why-content`

The goal is to define the lesson template we will use during content review without making every lesson feel identical.

## Decision

Use a consistent learning experience, not copy-paste lessons.

The learner should always feel:

- I know why this lesson matters.
- I understand one clear idea.
- I saw the idea in action.
- I practiced one small thing.
- I know what comes next.

But the opening, examples, visuals, mission type, and pacing may change by lesson type.

## Standard elements for most lessons

### 1. Hero / Orientation

Every lesson should start with a short orientation:

- What this lesson is about
- Why it matters now
- What the learner will be able to do after it

### 2. Tension / Problem

Most lessons should include a relatable problem or confusion.

The tone should be calm:

- Recognition, not fear
- Practical, not dramatic
- No shame

### 3. Core idea before glossary

Explain the main idea before loading the learner with terms.

Glossary should support understanding, not lead the lesson.

### 4. Limited glossary

Use only necessary terms.

Default:

- 1 to 3 terms per lesson
- English term explained immediately in simple Arabic
- Avoid dictionary-style lessons

### 5. One visual support

Most lessons should include one visual support when it helps.

Allowed:

- Screenshot
- Diagram
- Template
- Before/after
- Checklist
- Simple flow

Rule:

The visual must teach the idea, not promote the platform.

### 6. Video supports, not replaces

Video may appear after orientation and core context.

Rules:

- Lesson should still work if the video is skipped
- Video should reinforce one Aha
- Do not hide mission instructions only inside video

### 7. Light check before mission

A quick check can appear before the mission.

Default:

- 1 question
- Not an exam
- Confidence check, not grading pressure

Use more only when clearly justified.

### 8. Mission near the end

Mission appears only after the learner has enough context.

Follow:

- `MISSION_CONSTITUTION.md`
- `MISSION_TARGET_DESIGN.md`

### 9. Confidence close

Every lesson should end with a confidence close.

It should remind the learner:

- What they understood
- What they can now do
- What comes next

Do not end with:

- Platform promotion
- New heavy concept
- Technical warning
- Pressure

## Flexible elements

### Opening style may vary

Allowed:

- Direct promise
- Relatable situation
- Short story
- Question
- Before/after tension

### Visual type may vary

Intro lessons may use screenshots.

Creator lessons may use templates or before/after visuals.

Business lessons may use decision frames.

Analyst lessons may use simple tables or patterns.

Automator lessons may use flows.

Builder lessons may use diagrams and warnings.

### Mission type may vary

Use:

- Quick Check for light concept lessons
- Practice Mission for most lessons
- Capstone / Build Mission for module/path endings

### Lesson length may vary

Intro lessons should be lighter.

Practical path lessons can be richer.

Technical Builder lessons may be longer but must remain opt-in and beginner-safe.

## Anti-boredom rule

Consistency should reduce confusion, not create sameness.

Avoid:

- Same opening sentence every lesson
- Same mission format every lesson
- Same visual type every lesson
- Same example domain every lesson
- Same rhythm across a full path

Each lesson needs at least one distinct angle:

- Different situation
- Different example
- Different visual
- Different mission shape
- Different practical outcome

## Pilot decisions from Intro L1

Keep:

- Short Hero
- Calmer tension
- Core idea before glossary
- 2-term glossary
- One visual before quiz/mission
- 1-question quick check
- Warm mission with template
- Confidence close

Watch:

- Screenshot should not feel like assistant promotion
- Runtime still shows score/gating and must be redesigned later

## Pilot decisions from Creator L1

Keep:

- Practical Creator framing
- Content system vs posting as one Aha
- 3-term glossary max
- Manifesto mission with starter template
- One light quiz question
- Confidence close pointing to next lesson

Watch:

- Avoid funnel jargon overload
- Avoid platform-promo screenshots
- Keep business examples practical but not intimidating

## What this means for content review

When reviewing any lesson, check:

1. Is there one main Aha?
2. Does the lesson start with orientation?
3. Does the learner see the problem before the explanation?
4. Are terms introduced only when needed?
5. Is there visual support when helpful?
6. Is the quiz light?
7. Is the mission practice, not exam?
8. Does the lesson end with confidence?
9. Does this lesson have its own distinct angle?

## What not to do yet

Do not rewrite all lessons automatically.

Do not force every lesson into the exact same block sequence.

Do not redesign mission runtime yet.

Do not regenerate videos yet.

Do not seed assistant knowledge yet.
